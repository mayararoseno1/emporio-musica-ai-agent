# Data analysis

All six CSVs and every page of both PDFs were read before implementation. See data-audit.json for counts, columns, missing values and foreign-key checks. There are 65 products, 9 categories, 50 customers, 20 orders, 22 order lines and 25 promotions. No orphan foreign keys or duplicate rows were found. Product specs are JSON objects. IDs/quantities are integers; prices use Decimal; dates are ISO strings.

Order tracking and delivery estimates are legitimately missing for 12 orders; notes are missing for 16. Two order totals differ from quantities multiplied by current catalog prices. Historical unit prices are absent, so stored order totals remain authoritative and are never recomputed.

Policies are version 2.1, June 2025. They describe a larger catalog than the supplied 65-row snapshot: only the CSV is used for actual inventory. Promotion validity comes from is_active, never calendar campaign descriptions. Prices are BRL; discounts do not stack with PIX. Specific installment subsection 3.1 takes precedence over the generic table; ambiguous minimum for <=3 installments requires confirmation. Exactly R$500 shipping is undefined (above/below wording): refer to staff. Email and WhatsApp numbers conflict across sections: disclose the discrepancy instead of silently choosing. Receipt date, purchase channel and final-sale flags are absent from order tables: do not infer return eligibility from estimated delivery.

Structured tools serve catalog, stock, promotions and orders. PDF section retrieval serves store information, payment, returns, shipping, privacy and warranty. Customer records are never made into a searchable tool. Order access uses a trusted customer identity passed by the local operator, never an identity chosen by the LLM; production needs authentication. Data is a local snapshot, not a live inventory integration. No write operations are provided. Store policies are reported as supplied, not independently verified legal advice.
